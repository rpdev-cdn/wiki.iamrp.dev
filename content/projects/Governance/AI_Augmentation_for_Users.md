## **Enterprise Guardrails for Generative AI, Zero-Data Retention Tenants, Prompt Injection Defense & Human-in-the-Loop Accountability**

> [!abstract] Policy Statement
> The integration of Large Language Models (LLMs) and generative AI tools into enterprise workflows represents a significant leap in workforce productivity. However, unmanaged adoption introduces risks to data privacy, intellectual property, and operational integrity. This policy defines mandatory standards for safe AI usage, confidential data protection, prompt security, and continuous human-in-the-loop oversight.

---

## 1. Data Privacy and Corporate Confidentiality

The most critical operational risk associated with commercial AI tools is the inadvertent exposure of proprietary assets or protected records:
* **Data Classification Alignment**: All inputs must adhere strictly to the **[[Projects/Governance-and-Policies/Data_Classification_Policy|Data Classification Policy]]**.
* **Prohibition of Sensitive Data in Public AI**: Users are strictly barred from submitting Personally Identifiable Information (PII), proprietary source code, unreleased financial metrics, legal contracts, or trade secrets into public, multi-tenant AI systems.
* **Mandated Enterprise Sandboxes**: Enterprise workloads must exclusively utilize designated, enterprise-licensed AI endpoints that guarantee zero data retention (ZDR), isolated tenancy, and strict exclusion of customer prompts from foundational model training pools.

---

## 2. Validating AI Outputs & Mitigating Hallucinations

AI models generate statistically probable text that may appear authoritative while being factually inaccurate or synthetically fabricated:
* **The "Trust, But Verify" Standard**: AI outputs must be treated as initial drafts or unverified assertions requiring human verification.
* **Primary Source Reconciliation**: Numerical figures, legal citations, cryptographic parameters, and code snippets generated by AI must be cross-referenced against authoritative ground truth documents.
* **Bias & Logic Auditing**: Users must inspect AI responses for flawed premises, logical hallucinations, and exclusionary phrasing prior to publication or operational execution.

---

## 3. Prompt Security & Indirect Prompt Injection Defense

As AI assistants are granted access to live communication channels, external document repositories, and tool execution APIs, they become targets for adversarial prompt manipulation:
* **Indirect Prompt Injection Awareness**: Users and engineers must recognize how hostile instructions embedded within third-party PDFs, websites, or emails can hijack an AI agent's tool execution chain.
* **Structured System Constraints**: High-privilege agent prompts must enforce clear delimiter separation, role boundaries, and strict output schema validations.

---

## 4. Human-in-the-Loop Accountability

* **Non-Delegable Responsibility**: The ultimate legal and operational accountability for any code, document, communication, or analysis generated with AI assistance rests entirely with the individual human author.
* **Attribution & Transparency**: Internal teams must document when generative AI has contributed substantially to mission-critical infrastructure changes, security assessments, or production source code.
